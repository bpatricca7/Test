# Compact Walt Disney World resort collection

A series of small, cost-conscious LEGO® display kits, one for each Walt Disney World
resort hotel, built with the same pipeline as the large
[Riviera](../riviera-lego), [Grand Floridian](../grand-floridian-lego) and
[BoardWalk](../boardwalk-lego) models. Each kit shows only what makes the resort
recognisable: its entrance or central facade, its roof or tower silhouette, its
colours, and one or two small details. The large builds stay as they are; the
compact kits are separate folders named `<resort>-compact-lego`.

This file is the collection index: the lineup, the shared format, a completion
checklist and a comparison of cost and resale scenarios across all kits. The
lineup, checklist and comparison blocks are generated by
`python3 ../lego-kit/collection_report.py .` from [`lineup.csv`](lineup.csv) and the
kit folders; [`comparison.csv`](comparison.csv) holds the same numbers for a
spreadsheet.

## The lineup

21 kits: every Walt Disney World resort hotel owned and operated by Disney, as of
October 2026. A hotel and its Disney Vacation Club (DVC) villas on the same property
are one kit (for example Bay Lake Tower with the Contemporary, Kidani Village with
Animal Kingdom Lodge), because the compact model shows the part of the property
that makes it recognisable.

## The compact format

Every kit uses the same display and presentation (`lego-kit/compact.py`):

- **Base:** 24 × 16 studs (19.2 × 12.8 cm) of dark bluish gray, with a two-row black
  band along the front as a plinth.
- **Building:** the signature building behind the band, at about 1:250. One storey
  is 4 plates: a course of bricks with the windows, then a plate band. That is the
  scale of the large resort models.
- **Details:** at most one or two in front (a palm, a lamp, a tree, a sign post).
- **Ground:** lawns are green plates with their studs showing; paths, paving, sand
  and water are tiles.
- **Shared assemblies:**
  - storey blocks (`block()`) and the standard palm (`palm()`);
  - the same short list of lawn plates and ground tiles in every kit;
  - the same Bestseller roof slopes.
- **Same renders and booklet:** the same five finished views, the same booklet
  layout and the same README sections. Each kit's README is written by
  `lego-kit/kit_readme.py` from its own files.
- **Targets:** about 300–600 pieces and $40–60 of parts per kit, smaller where the
  model still looks right. These are design targets, not verified prices.

Sourcing rules: only elements in Pick a Brick's Bestseller range that are still in
2025–2026 LEGO sets (fast US shipping, up to 999 of an element per order). Every
kit's `export_parts.py` step refuses to finish if a part breaks this rule. No
custom or printed parts, and no rare colours.

Every kit includes:
- **Model:** the editable design (`design.py`) and the LDraw model (`model/*.mpd`).
- **Checks:** collisions, connections, assembly order and stability, written to
  `checks.md`.
- **Renders and instructions:** finished-model renders and a step-by-step PDF
  booklet.
- **Parts and ordering:**
  - a bill of materials with element IDs, design IDs and colours;
  - Pick a Brick upload files for 1, 10 and 25 kits;
  - a retry file, a BrickLink wanted list and a Rebrickable import;
  - a cost sheet.
- **README:** dimensions, piece and part counts, signature features and cost
  assumptions.

## Cost and resale comparison

Parts costs are the last Pick a Brick prices in the offline data (LEGO's 2022
Bestseller list and a late-2025 listing). lego.com, BrickLink and Rebrickable
could not be reached from the environment these kits were made in, so **none of
these prices was checked live**.

On September 14, 2026 LEGO raised about a third of Pick a Brick prices, by about
17% on average, with tiles and small parts hit hardest. The "2026 est." column
adds 10% to the listed prices, and each kit's README also shows a +20% stress
case. Upload a kit's 10-kit file to Pick a Brick to get the real total before
pricing anything.

The landed cost per kit includes:
- parts at the 2026 estimate;
- $3.00 of packaging: a box, bags and a card with a link to the PDF booklet;
- labour at $15/hour: 3 seconds per piece to count and bag, plus 6 minutes to pack;
- $10.50 of USPS Ground Advantage postage when the seller pays shipping.

Etsy's US fees are $0.20 per listing, 6.5% of the order and 3% + $0.25 for payment
processing.

For context: LEGO's own display sets usually sell for about 7–12¢ a piece. These
kits cost 10–15¢ a piece in parts alone at Pick a Brick prices, so they have to
sell on being unique, not on price per piece.

The table uses one price point per kit, twice the 2026 parts estimate.
Each kit's README shows 1.6×, 2× and 2.5×, with and without free shipping. All of
these numbers are set in [`lego-kit/pricing.py`](../lego-kit/pricing.py) and can be
changed there.

## Digital checks vs. a physical prototype

Every kit is checked on the computer:
- no two parts share space;
- every part is held by a stud;
- each model is one connected piece;
- in step order, nothing is pressed on from below onto a part already placed, and
  nothing stays loose for long;
- no large part hangs on a single stud.

These checks can't test clutch, the strength of clips and bars, weight and balance,
or how easy a step is for a person. **No physical prototype has been built for any
kit yet.** Before selling a kit, order its parts once, build it from the printed
booklet and fix anything the build shows up.

## Before you sell: trademarks and names

The resort names, and some resorts' look, are Disney trademarks and designs. The
kits use the names to describe what they're inspired by, with an "unofficial fan
design" note on every booklet. Selling them is a different matter from building
them:
- **Disney can object:** an unofficial product that uses Disney names in its title
  or listing can draw a takedown or a legal claim, even with a disclaimer.
- **LEGO's trademark:** "LEGO" shouldn't appear in a shop name or product title.
  "Built with genuine LEGO® elements" in the description is the usual wording.
- **No characters:** the kits for the character-themed Value resorts (All-Star
  Movies, Art of Animation, Pop Century) use generic icons only, and are less
  recognisable as a result.
- **Listings:** get advice from an IP lawyer before listing these for sale. A safe
  route is a generic product name, for example "Victorian Beach Resort, compact
  brick kit", plus a description of what it's inspired by.

This is not legal advice.
