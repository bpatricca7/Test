Statistical study · November 2026

Instagram reach is a threshold game. Small gains in watch time and sends compound into large gains in views.

This study combines what Instagram has said about its ranking systems, benchmark studies covering more than 100 million posts, and a simulation of how test-then-expand distribution turns viewer behaviour into views. It ends with a prioritized playbook, a 30-day plan, and a script you can run on your own data.

Key findings, ranked by expected impact

Ranked by how much each lever is likely to move views for a typical creator, using the evidence in the sections below.

  1. The first three seconds decide most of a Reel's fate.DatasetModel

    Socialinsider measured skip rates of 60.5–65.5% across 9.8M Reels. Watch time is one of Instagram's top three signals. In the model, raising watch time 10% raises typical views 27%.

  2. Make things people send to a specific friend.Instagram

    Mosseri names watch time, likes and sends as the top three signals. Sends per reach matter more for reaching people who don't follow you; likes matter more with followers. Discovery runs on sends.

  3. Reach responds faster than quality improves.Model

    Each wave of distribution is a pass/fail test, so improvements multiply. A 25% gain across the three signals gives 3.8× typical views and 4.6× the chance of a 100k-view post. Across 13 alternative model settings it ranges from 2.9× to 7.3×.

  4. Originality is a hard gate, not a bonus.Instagram

    Accounts that repost others' content 10+ times in 30 days lose recommendations. Since April 30, 2026 this covers photos and carousels, not only Reels. Other apps' watermarks and low-resolution video reduce discoverability.

  5. Use Reels to reach new people and carousels to deepen engagement.Dataset

    Reels reach 1.3–1.4× as many people as carousels and 2.3–3.1× as many as single images. Carousels lead engagement per person reached (6.9% vs 3.3%) and earn about 9× the saves of images.

  6. Post more often, but only as often as quality holds.DatasetModel

    Across 2.1M posts, 3–5 posts a week gave about 12% more reach per post than 1–2, and roughly double the follower growth. In the model, if quality slips 4% for each extra weekly post past three, the best rate is 3–4 a week.

  7. Keep your first audience matched to your content.ModelInstagram

    A post is first scored on a small group, so a mismatched one sinks it early. In the model, a 50%-matched first wave cuts typical views by 86%. Bought followers, engagement pods and abrupt niche pivots all create that mismatch. Trial Reels let you test a new direction on non-followers instead.

  8. Small accounts are not locked out.InstagramDataset

    Instagram tests public posts from small creators on people who don't follow them. Accounts with 1–5K followers reach 6.65% of their follower count per post, against 3.5% for 100K–1M accounts.

  9. Hashtags are not a reach lever. Keywords are.InstagramDataset

    Posts have been capped at 5 hashtags since December 2025. In Metricool's 24.4M-post study, posts with hashtags got 31.7% fewer views (a correlation, not proof of cause). Put searchable words in the caption and the on-screen text instead.

  10. Timing is a weak lever.Dataset

    Large studies disagree on the best hour (Buffer: Thursday 9am; Later: 5am; Metricool: 8pm), and Metricool calls the differences between days minimal. Post when your followers are active, then stop worrying about it.

  11. Most creator "tests" cannot detect anything.Math

    Views vary so much from post to post that detecting a real 50% improvement takes about 140–380 posts per version when you compare different videos. Testing two hooks on the same video cuts that by 2–4×.

  12. Judge by the median, not the average or your best post.ModelMath

    The mean is about 9× the median and the top tenth of posts deliver 77% of views, so one outlier swamps the average. 65% of Reel views arrive within 3 days, so wait that long before judging a post.

How Instagram decides who sees a post

There isn't one Instagram algorithm. Feed, Stories, Explore, Reels and Search each run their own ranking system with their own signals, and posts from accounts you follow are ranked separately from recommendations Instagram. Each system predicts how likely you are to take certain actions on a post, such as watching it, watching to the end, liking it, sending it to a friend, or commenting. It then combines those predictions into a score.

Distribution happens in steps. A post is shown to a small group first: some followers, and for public accounts, people who don't follow you. If it does well against competing content, it moves to a bigger audience, and the cycle repeats Instagram. Trial Reels make this explicit by showing a Reel to non-followers first, then giving you its metrics after about 24 hours.

Meta's Transparency Center "system cards" list other predictions too, such as using a Reel's audio, swiping through a carousel, commenting and following. Meta says it withholds signals that could be gamed, and that the signals change often.

Viewers now steer this too. Since December 2025, "Your Algorithm" lets people add and remove topics, first on Reels and later on Explore and Feed. A post that is clearly about a recognizable topic is easier to route to people who chose that topic, which is another reason to keep a consistent niche.

The gates: content that won't be recommended at all

Ranking only happens if a post is eligible. Instagram's Recommendations Guidelines hold recommended content to a higher bar than its Community Guidelines. Ineligible content still reaches followers but is never shown to non-followers, and if an account is ineligible, none of its posts are recommended Instagram.

  • Unoriginal content. Accounts that post others' content 10+ times in 30 days without materially changing it are removed from recommendations. Since April 30, 2026 this applies to photos and carousels as well as Reels. Adding a border, watermark, speed change, new caption or credit does not make content original. An account becomes eligible again once most of its recent posts are original.
  • Visible quality issues. Reels that are blurry or low-resolution, carry another app's watermark, or are mostly text are less discoverable. Your own logo is fine.
  • Undisclosed AI personas. Since August 31, 2026, accounts built around an AI-generated person that don't use the "AI-generated profile" label get limited recommendations. Using AI to edit photos or captions doesn't require the label.
  • Check Account Status (Settings → Account Status). It shows whether your content can be recommended, and lets you remove or appeal posts that broke the guidelines.

Buffer's design compares each account with itself in weeks when it posted more versus fewer times. That removes the obvious confound that bigger, better-resourced accounts post more. The result is diminishing but positive returns, plus a "no-post penalty": weeks with no posts grew less than the account's usual rate. Metricool found the best rate depends on account size: small accounts did best at 7–14 posts a week, and accounts over 100K followers got more interactions per post when they posted less often.

This fits Instagram's statement that small creators' public posts get tested on non-followers. It also fits the model: test audiences are partly a fixed non-follower batch, so a small account's first wave is large relative to its follower count.

Hashtags and timing

Hashtags. Metricool found that posts with at least one hashtag got 31.7% fewer views and 33.9% fewer interactions than average. That is a correlation, and accounts that lean on hashtags may differ in other ways. Still, no large dataset supports hashtags as a reach strategy. Instagram now indexes captions for keyword search and caps posts at 5 hashtags.

Timing. Buffer (9.6M posts) found the best slots were Thursday 9am, Wednesday 12pm and Wednesday 6pm. Later (6M+ posts) found 5am. Metricool found 6–9pm, peaking at 8pm. When studies this large disagree, the true effect is probably small and specific to each audience. Use your own Insights to see when your followers are online.

The bar is rising

Organic reach is falling across the platform. Socialinsider's Instagram reach rate fell 14% year over year to August 2026. Metricool's 40M-post study reported Reels reach down 35% year over year. Buffer's median engagement per person reached fell from about 7.3% in 2024 to 5.4% in 2025. More content is competing for the same attention, so the score needed to pass each wave keeps rising. That makes the convex, threshold dynamics in the next section more important, not less.

Reading these numbers. Socialinsider and Rival IQ divide engagement by followers (about 0.3–0.6%); Buffer divides by reach (about 3–7%). Socialinsider reports means, which viral outliers inflate; Buffer and Emplifi report medians. All samples are the vendors' own customers, mostly brands. Instagram redefined "views" in 2025, so figures from before and after don't compare cleanly.

The reach model: why small gains compound

The statements and datasets show what matters, but not how much. To answer that, this study simulates test-then-expand distribution. Each simulated post goes to a first wave of viewers. The system measures the wave's watch time, sends and likes against competing content, weighting them in Mosseri's stated order. A post that clears the bar moves to a wave 3.5× larger, where the bar is a little higher and the audience a little less closely matched. A post that misses is throttled and stops. Every wave has realistic sampling noise.

The structure follows Instagram's statements. The constants (wave sizes, weights, the bar) are assumptions, because Instagram doesn't publish them. So every result below is stated relative to a baseline creator, and was re-checked under five different signal weightings and eight sets of platform constants. Only conclusions that hold across all thirteen are reported as findings. The baseline creator has 5,000 followers, a median of 1,200 views per post, and a 1.6% chance of any one post passing 100k views.

The curve bends upward because each wave is a pass/fail test, and passing one opens a bigger, harder one. A post that is 10% better clears more waves, and every extra wave multiplies its audience 3.5×. The same logic works in reverse: a 20% drop in quality cuts typical views by about two-thirds.

Your first audience is the first gate

This is the mechanism behind several "don'ts." Bought followers, follow-for-follow, engagement pods and sudden niche changes all put people who don't care about the content into the first wave. Those viewers don't watch, send or like, so the post fails the first test before the recommender can find its real audience. A 50%-matched first wave cuts typical views from 1,324 to 186 (−86%) and the chance of a 10k post from 15% to 3.6%.

If quality holds, posting more is close to free upside, and Buffer's within-account data suggests it often does hold. The measurement trap in your Insights

Insights reports rates per account reached, measured on whoever actually saw the post. Posts that travel far are measured on colder audiences, which pulls their rates down. In the model, the true quality of a post predicts its views with a rank correlation of 0.84, but the rates you can see in Insights correlate less:

Don't conclude that a signal doesn't matter because its correlation in your Insights is weak. Among the rates you can see, sends per reach is the most faithful proxy for quality, which matches Instagram's advice to track it.

Watch-time math: hook, hold and length

A retention curve has two parts. First comes the hook: the share of viewers who are still there after about 3 seconds, which is one minus the skip rate. After that comes a steadier decline, described by its half-life: the number of seconds it takes for half of the remaining viewers to leave. Skip rate and the retention chart are both in Reels Insights.

  • The hook multiplies everything. Going from a 62% to a 50% skip rate keeps 32% more viewers past second 3 (50% instead of 38%), and every later second of watch time grows by the same 32%. No other edit has that much leverage.
  • The half-life sets the right length. Instagram predicts both how long people watch and whether they finish. If the score rewards both, the best length scales with your half-life. Under three plausible ways of combining them, it lands between 1 and 2.75 half-lives after the hook. A creator whose viewers drift off fast should make short Reels; one with a 40-second half-life can go long.

Rules for testing

  • Pair your tests. Publish the same video with two different hooks or openings, for example one as a Trial Reel. Shared content removes most of the noise: in the model the two versions' log views correlate at 0.77, which cuts the posts you need by 2–4×.
  • Test big swings. A new hook style or a new format can plausibly double views, and doubling is detectable in 20–80 posts. Tweaks like caption wording or posting time can't be measured on one account.
  • Compare like with like. Mosseri's advice is to compare Trial Reels only with other Trial Reels, because they almost always get less reach than regular posts.
  • Use leading indicators. Skip rate and sends per reach respond within a day and vary less than views. Use them to choose between versions, and use median views to confirm over a month.
  • Write down the test before you run it, and decide what result would change your behaviour. Otherwise, looking at many metrics will always turn up something that seems to have worked.

The playbook

Ordered by expected impact. Each step names the signal it moves.

1. Win the first three seconds Dataset

  • Open on the payoff, the problem, or the most surprising frame. Never on a logo, an intro or "hey guys."
  • Show motion in the first frame, and put the promise in on-screen text of seven words or fewer, so muted viewers get it too.
  • Cut the first half-second of dead air from every clip.
  • Track your skip rate per Reel. The datasets average 60–65%, so anything below your own median is a win worth repeating.

2. Earn sends Instagram

  • Before you publish, ask: who would send this, and to whom? If there's no clear answer, rework the post.
  • People send things that are about someone they know ("the friend who always…"), useful to keep (a checklist or how-to), surprising, or an opinion that says something about who they are.
  • Sends drive reach to non-followers, so they're the main lever for growth. Likes mostly reflect how your existing followers feel.

3. Hold attention, then choose the length Model

  • Cut every pause. Change something on screen every 2–4 seconds. Save the payoff for late in the video, and design endings that loop back into the start.
  • Read your retention chart to estimate your half-life, then aim for 1–2.75 half-lives after the hook. The datasets' overall sweet spot is 30–60 seconds, but your own curve should override that.

4. Stay original and clean Instagram

  • Post your own footage, or transform other material substantially. Crediting the creator does not make a repost original.
  • Export without other apps' watermarks, at full resolution, with no black borders.
  • If an AI-generated person is the face of the account, use the AI-generated profile label.
  • Check Account Status monthly.

5. Use each format for its job Dataset

  • Reels for discovery; carousels, ideally 8 or more slides, for saves and depth. Use single images sparingly: they trail on both reach and engagement, and Metricool measured their reach falling 22% in a year.

6. Post often, protect quality DatasetModel

  • Start at 3–5 posts a week, then add more only while your median sends per reach and skip rate hold steady.
  • Never skip a week entirely.
  • Use Trial Reels to post more experiments without crowding your followers' feeds.

7. Protect your first audience Model

  • Never buy followers, likes or views, and never join pods or follow-for-follow schemes.
  • Keep a recognizable niche.
  • Test new directions as Trial Reels on non-followers before you pivot your main feed.

8. Borrow a second first audience with collabs Dataset

  • Collab posts show your post to a partner's followers as well as yours. Emplifi reported that small brands' collab posts got several times the interactions of their regular posts, though brands that collaborate tend to be more active, which inflates that figure.
  • Choose partners whose audience matches your content, for the first-wave reasons above.

9. Write for search, not hashtags Instagram

  • Put plain, searchable phrases in the caption's first line and in the on-screen text.
  • Use up to 5 specific hashtags for categorization, not for reach.
  • Ask a real question when you want comments. Metricool found questions go with 37% more comments.

A 30-day plan

Week 1

Measure your baseline

Post at your normal rate. Export Insights and run the script below. Write down your median views, skip rate, sends per reach, and your sd of log views.

Week 2

Hook sprint

For 4–6 videos, publish two openings of the same video, one as a Trial Reel. Compare skip rate and sends within each pair. Keep the winning hook style.

Week 3

Send sprint

For every post, write one line saying who sends it to whom. Kill drafts without a clear answer. Compare median sends per reach with week 1.

Week 4

Scale what worked

Add 1–2 posts a week using the winning patterns. Re-run the script. Keep the changes that moved the median, and drop the ones that didn't.

Run the analysis on your own account

The repository includes analyze_my_account.py. Export post-level insights as CSV (Meta Business Suite → Insights → Content → Export, or the Professional Dashboard), then run:

cd instagram_algorithm_study
pip install numpy pandas
python3 analyze_my_account.py my_export.csv      # writes my_account_report.md
python3 analyze_my_account.py --demo             # labelled synthetic example

It runs on your computer and sends your data nowhere. The report gives you:

  • Your view distribution: median with a bootstrap confidence interval, typical views, sd of log views, and the share of views from your top 10% of posts.
  • Median views by format and length, with confidence intervals, so you can see which differences are real.
  • Which of your signals (sends, saves, likes, comments, follows per reach, watch time, skip rate) track your views most closely.
  • A regression of log views on things you control (format, length, hashtag count, caption length, posting hour, gap since your last post, time trend), with robust standard errors.
  • How many posts you would need to detect a given improvement, based on your own variability.

The other scripts reproduce every model number in this study: funnel_model.py (the reach model and robustness checks) and experiment_design.py (power analysis and the retention model).

Methods and limits

  • No internal data. Instagram doesn't publish its ranking weights or post-level data for other accounts, and scraping is against its terms. Everything here comes from Instagram's own statements, published third-party datasets, and simulation.
  • Verification. Instagram statements were checked against reporting that quotes Instagram or Mosseri directly. This environment's network policy blocked opening some primary pages in full, including socialinsider.io, buffer.com and transparency.meta.com. Those figures come from the publishers' pages as surfaced by search, and key ones were cross-checked against independent coverage. Re-check exact decimals before quoting them in public.
  • Datasets are observational and drawn from each vendor's customers, mostly brands. They show associations. Buffer's frequency study is the strongest design here because it compares each account with itself.
  • The model is a model. Its structure matches Instagram's description; its constants are assumptions tuned to give realistic view distributions. Conclusions are reported only when they hold across 13 variants: five signal weightings, and eight sets of wave sizes, audience cooling, bar steepness and test-batch size. The JavaScript simulator on this page reproduces the Python model (typical views 1,328 vs 1,324 at baseline).
  • Things change. Instagram changes ranking often. This study reflects statements and data available on November 4, 2026.
