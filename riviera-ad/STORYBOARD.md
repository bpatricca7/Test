# 94 Steps Back to the Riviera

**Concept:** "One we stayed in. One we built." The parent who took the holiday photos tells the story. The real Riviera Resort and the brick model appear side by side.
**Tagline:** *94 steps back to the Riviera.*
**CTA:** Step 1 starts at **brickcoodle.com**
**Format:** 9:16 vertical, 1080×1920, 30 fps, 30.0 s, loops. A 4:5 feed cut uses the same layout.
**Sound:** French Riviera pop-house at 120 BPM in D major. The music has two stop-downs, and brick "snap" sounds work as percussion. The voice is Kokoro `af_heart` (warm, first-person parent) with one French "Voilà !" from `ff_siwis`.

---

## The story in one breath
We stayed at the Riviera Resort and nobody wanted to leave, so we took a little piece of it home: the **Riviera Resort brick model**. It has **1,746 pieces** and every detail lines up with the real place. The only difference is that this one fits on a shelf. **Every step is illustrated and every part is listed**, and the build takes six to eight hours of "pass me the red one!". *94 steps back to the Riviera.* **Step 1 starts at brickcoodle.com.**

## Why it works
1. **It hooks in the first frame.** The first frame is already a split screen: the family's real photo of the arched entrance on top, the brick version below, and "ONE WE STAYED IN." on screen. The voice starts at 0.02 s, and "ONE WE BUILT." lands with a brick snap at 1.5 s. Place, product and promise all read in about a second, with the sound on or off.
2. **It is a real family memory, not an ad.** The client's own photos, shot from behind the family, are narrated by the parent who took them. That keeps the ad warm and native to the feed, and the family is never named.
3. **The photo turns into the model on screen.** The balcony photo turns into bricks during a silent stop-down. It then match-cuts on the music drop and "Voilà !" into the rendering, with the canopy staying in the same spot and at the same size. The full model is on screen at 8 s.
4. **It proves the likeness and makes a joke of the scale.** A quick REAL vs BRICK slider (mansard roof, then the whole façade) ends on "This one fits on a shelf." with the real dimensions, 38 × 26 cm · 16 cm tall.
5. **It shows what the buyer actually gets.** The booklet cover, a real step page and the parts inventory make it clear this is a build with instructions. After that, the model builds itself from step 1 to 94 on the beat, with a live step counter.
6. **It sounds like a family.** "Six to eight hours of… pass me the red one!" turns the build time into a family scene.
7. **The CTA follows naturally.** "94 steps back to the Riviera." is followed by "Step 1 starts at brickcoodle.com", so clicking reads as the first step of the build. The URL sits on a blue brick button that pulses with the booklet's orange "new part" outline and stays on screen for 5 s. The last frame drops back into the opening split screen, so the loop is seamless.

## What we took from each concept
- **Real vs Brick** (highest score, used as the base): the split-screen hook "ONE WE STAYED IN. / ONE WE BUILT." with REAL/BRICK chips and the red slider handle; the slider-wipe comparison window with a tally; the payoff "This one fits on a shelf." with a dimension line; "Every step illustrated. Every part listed." over pages dealt like cards; the "Step 1 starts at" CTA framing; the divider drop that loops back to the opening; no red paired with yellow.
- **Family Story:** the parent narrating from behind the camera; the instant prints on a cream table with washi tape that echoes the girl's lilac tee; the handwritten caption; the photo-to-stud brickify during a silent stop-down; the music dropping on the French "Voilà !"; "Six to eight hours of… pass me the red one!" with a handwritten label and arrow to the red flag; the end-card line "Step-by-step instructions + full parts list"; a sky-only cloud plate from the balcony photo.
- **Satisfying Build:** the tagline "94 steps back to the Riviera."; the build locked to the baseplate with a step counter; skipping the close-up and different-camera steps; the orange "new part" outline used as the CTA pulse and as trace boxes on the pages; the cover cropped above its fine print; the trip polaroid leaning on the model; the crossfade idea toward the client's rendering, which now leads the end card.
- **TikTok Hype:** text on screen at the first frame with no fade; the match cut from the real canopy to the brick canopy on the drop; the 0 → 1,746 counter on the drop; two musical stop-downs; the brick-snap montage with rising pitch and a "STEP NN/94" counter.

## Compliance checklist
- **Product naming:** it is always called the "Riviera Resort brick model". Our copy never uses "set", "kit", "box", "included" or "official", and gives no price.
- **LEGO and Disney:** our copy never mentions LEGO, Disney or BrickLink. The only exception is the booklet cover's own small descriptive subtitle, which appears as part of the page art.
- **Disclaimer:** exact text, 24 px, high contrast, on screen from 22.0 s to the last frame (8 s).
- **URL:** always shown as **brickcoodle.com**. It is on screen for 5 s and spoken once.
- **Theme-park dome and shuttle bus:** cropped out of every frame of the balcony photo. Only the sky (above the horizon) is reused, as a soft cloud backdrop.
- **Booklet pages:** the cover is shown only cropped above its fine print. The price page (page 74) and the "About this model" page (page 2) are never shown.
- **Arrival photo:** the white strip at the top is cropped off, and the camera never pushes in on bystanders or signage.
- **Claims:** all of them come from the booklet: 1,746 pieces, 94 illustrated steps, 38 × 26 cm, 16 cm tall, about 6–8 hours, full parts list.
- **Loop and 4:5:** the last frame loops back to the first. All text sits inside the safe zone (x 80–940, y 290–1495), so the 4:5 feed cut keeps every line.
